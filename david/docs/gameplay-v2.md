The bar: the third-person action of a top game of today (the feel of aiming and hitting, the weight of every move,
readable animation, juicy feedback) — on desktop (mouse + keyboard) and on phones (touch), in Hebrew, faithful to the

## 1 · The sling is carried, drawn and stowed (never hanging from his hand)

* **Carried:** folded and tucked under his sash (the girdle, חֲגוֹרָה) at the right hip — the two cords doubled over,
  the leather pouch and the cords' ends hanging a hand's breadth below the sash. It moves only as a tucked strap of
  leather and cord would: a small, damped sway with his gait (no jumping, no flailing, never through his legs). This
  is how a shepherd carries a sling while walking (in his girdle or wound round the waist); 1 Sam 17:40 "וְקַלְעוֹ
  בְיָדוֹ" describes it in his hand as he goes out to fight — in the hand only when it is about to be used.
* **Drawn** when the player starts to aim (the sling button): the right hand pulls it from the sash, the cords fall
  open, the hand dips into the shepherd's bag (the יַלְקוּט at his hip, 1 Sam 17:40) for a stone and lays it in the
  pouch, the fingers take the cords' ends (the loop on the middle finger, the knot between thumb and forefinger), and
  the whirl begins — ≈0.6–0.8 s from the press to the first turn, fluid and readable; the aim camera eases in at the
  same time so it never feels like lag. While he stays "armed" (aiming again within a few seconds) only the reload
  (a stone from the bag into the pouch) runs between throws.
* **Stowed** a few seconds after the last throw, or at once when he runs, climbs, carries the lamb, picks something up
  or a cinematic starts: the hand folds the sling and tucks it back under the sash (≈0.4 s).
* The staff stays in his left hand throughout (as now); every hand pose is a real grip (no fingers through cords or
  through the staff).

## 2 · The five smooth stones (more realistic)

"וַיִּבְחַר־לוֹ חֲמִשָּׁה חַלֻּקֵי־אֲבָנִים מִן־הַנַּחַל וַיָּשֶׂם אֹתָם בִּכְלִי הָרֹעִים אֲשֶׁר־לוֹ וּבַיַּלְקוּט" (1 Sam 17:40) —
he CHOOSES them: smooth, round stones from the stream bed, into the shepherd's bag.

* The wadi bed is full of pebbles of every shape; the smooth, rounded ones the right size for a sling are fewer and
  must be found (they read on close look: rounder, polished, a wet sheen where the trickle runs) — no glowing
  beacons; a subtle, natural cue when he is close (and a gentle hint if the player searches for long).
* Picking is a real action: David crouches or goes down on one knee in the stream bed, reaches into the gravel or the
  water, lifts the stone, turns it in his fingers and looks at it, rubs it clean (or rinses it in the water), and puts
  it into the bag at his hip (the flap opens, the stone goes in) — ≈1.3–1.8 s, with the camera easing closer while he
  kneels. A rough or flat stone he examines and tosses back (a short, natural reaction, a line of text in Hebrew such
  as לֹא חָלָק דַּיּוֹ), so choosing matters. The counter counts only good stones (5).
* Good stones fly truer: the stones he chose are his best ammunition (the practice and the fight use the bag; when it
  runs low he refills from the stone heap of the practice range or the stream bed — see 3).

## 3 · The sling practice — a real, smart, fun challenge

The core: hitting must be **skill**, not "point the camera and the game solves the trajectory". Aiming, timing and
judging distance and wind are what make it fun to master.

* **Ballistics:** the stone flies on a real trajectory (gravity, a little drag, wind at range). No auto-solve onto the
  reticle: the reticle shows the launch direction; the player learns the drop (aim higher at range) and the drift.
  Aim assist only as a small magnetism on touch screens.
* **Power and timing:** the longer the whirl, the faster the stone (as now), but the release must be TIMED — the
  release point of the whirl decides the direction: released in the sweet window the stone goes where he aims; early
  or late it pulls left/right and high/low. The window narrows as the whirl speeds up (more power = more range and a
  flatter shot, but harder to time). A clear, elegant timing cue on the HUD (e.g. a pulse on the reticle ring at the
  whirl's period, the window lit), a distinct sound and flash for a perfect release.
* **The range** (in the wadi below the pasture, set up by David himself — clay jars, gourds, a hanging waterskin, a
  pottery shard on a stick: Iron Age shepherds' things; no bullseyes, no modern targets): a short course of rounds that
  escalate —
  1. warm-up: three jars on a terrace wall at 12–16 m (learn the timing);
  2. range: jars and gourds at 22–35 m on the far bank (learn the drop), with a light wind shown by the grass, dust and
     the hanging cloth;
  3. moving: a gourd swinging on a cord under a terebinth branch, a jar on a log rolling down the slope (lead the
     target);
  4. the finale, "אֶל־הַשַּׂעֲרָה וְלֹא יַחֲטִא" (Judg 20:16, already in the catalog): a thin cord holding a hanging
     waterskin/jar at ≈20 m — hit the cord and the target drops.
  Each round has a small target count and a score (hits, perfect releases, streaks, stones used, time) with a
  three-step rating; failing a round never blocks the story (retry or continue after a few tries), and the player
  can always come back to the range to beat the score.
* **Juice:** the stone's whistle and a short trail at high speed, a slow-motion beat on the last hit of a round, jars
  bursting into sherds with dust and the water inside spraying, gourds splitting, the cord snapping and the skin
  falling, a hit-marker, a streak counter, short Hebrew praise lines (narration-style, never verses), the camera
  following the stone on a perfect long shot (briefly, skippable).
* Phones: the same mechanics with touch (hold the sling button to whirl, drag to aim, release to throw); the timing cue
  large enough to read; nothing important under the thumbs.

## 4 · The bear — realistic animation and a realistic, fun fight (the next wave)

The verse is the fight's structure (1 Sam 17:34–35): "וּבָא הָאֲרִי וְאֶת־הַדּוֹב וְנָשָׂא שֶׂה מֵהָעֵדֶר · וְיָצָאתִי
אַחֲרָיו וְהִכִּתִיו וְהִצַּלְתִּי מִפִּיו וַיָּקָם עָלַי וְהֶחֱזַקְתִּי בִּזְקָנוֹ וְהִכִּתִיו וַהֲמִיתִּיו".

* **The animal:** the Syrian brown bear (Ursus arctos syriacus — the bear of the Land of Israel in antiquity): pale
  straw-brown, a shoulder hump, a heavy head carried low, plantigrade hind feet (the whole sole on the ground), a
  rolling, heavy walk; a gallop that bounds; it rears up on its hind legs to see or to threaten; its warnings before a
  charge: huffing, jaw-popping, the head swinging low, the ears back, a stomp of the forepaws. Animation must be
  believable from the gameplay camera and close up: weight, follow-through, foot contacts, the spine and the hump
  working, the loose skin and fur, the head and jaw.
* **"וְיָצָאתִי אַחֲרָיו"** — the chase: the bear carries the lamb toward the thicket; David runs after it; sling hits
  slow it (a hit to the hindquarters makes it stumble, turn and snarl).
* **"וְהִכִּתִיו וְהִצַּלְתִּי מִפִּיו"** — cornered at the thicket's edge, the lamb in its jaws: a well-timed blow of the
  staff to the snout (or a stone to the head) makes it open its jaws; he pulls the lamb free and it bolts back to the
  flock.
* **"וַיָּקָם עָלַי"** — it rises against him: the bear rears up to its full height and comes at him (the camera low,
  the roar).
* **The fight** (realistic, readable, fair, fun): the bear swipes with either forepaw (telegraphed by the weight shift
  and the shoulder), lunges to bite, charges (some charges are bluffs that stop short — real bear behaviour), rears
  and slams down; David keeps his distance with the staff, dodges sideways out of a charge (never straight back),
  jabs the staff at the face and the nose (the sensitive spot), strikes the head when it overreaches or after a missed
  charge, uses the sling when it backs off, and uses the rocks and the tree trunks to break a charge. A hit on David
  hurts (he can be knocked down and must get up); the bear tires as the fight goes on and its attacks get slower and
  more desperate. Counter-windows after its attacks reward reading it; mashing does not work.
* **"וְהֶחֱזַקְתִּי בִּזְקָנוֹ"** — only after he has fought it well (the bear exhausted and staggered by enough good
  hits): at the next lunge, a timed move — he sidesteps the open jaws and seizes it by the jaw: Targum Yonatan
  renders the words "וַאֲחָדִית בְּלוֹעֵיהּ" (I seized him by his jaw) and Radak explains that he did not hold the hair of
  the beard alone "אלא הזקן עם הלחי התחתון" — the beard together with the lower jaw. So David grips the fur under the
  bear's chin together with its lower jaw, forces its head up and aside (a short struggle the player must hold), and
  **"וְהִכִּתִיו וַהֲמִיתִּיו"** — strikes it down with the staff. Cinematic, slow motion at the grip, the verse on
  screen (the catalog's `s1_17_35_beard`), no blood, no gore.
* **After:** the lamb trembling, lifted onto his shoulders (as now), carried back to the flock.

## Accuracy

* Every verse on screen comes only from the catalog `src/content/sources.ts` (verseArgs / quoteText / sourceRef);
  `python3 tools/sources/verify_sources.py --quiet` must exit 0. UI lines and praise lines are narration-style Hebrew
  (pointed where shown in the film's style), never styled as verses, never quotation marks around non-quotes.
* The sling: two cords and a leather pouch (as now); the stones: smooth wadi pebbles (5–7 cm, ≈100–200 g); no metal
  targets, no modern objects.

### The five smooth stones — quick and pleasant (replaces §2's rejects)

* **Every stone he can pick up is a good one** (smooth, round, the right size). No rough or flat stones to examine and
  toss back, no "not smooth enough" lines; the bed's gravel stays as scenery only.
* **Scattered widely, easy to see:** 9-10 smooth stones spread along the whole visible stretch of the stream bed and its
  edges (not in one patch) — each lying on top of the gravel or at the water's edge, a little larger, paler and more
  polished than the gravel, with a wet sheen; a soft natural glint of the sun on the nearest ones. A first-time player
  finds five in about 30-60 s.
* **Quick to take:** the prompt from ≈1.8 m in any facing; the pick ≈1.0-1.2 s (down, take it, a short look as he
  rises, into the bag), the camera barely moves; he can walk on at once. A gentle pointer to the nearest stone after
  ≈20 s without a find.

### The sling — power by skill, a fair challenge (replaces §3's release timing)

* **Hold to whirl, release to throw — at any moment.** No timing window on the whirl and no penalty for the moment of
  release: the stone always leaves the pouch at the right point of the circle (the animation adapts within ≈0.1 s).
* **Power is the skill:** while he whirls, a clear power gauge round the reticle fills (≈1 s from weak to full); a weak
  throw is slow and drops short, a strong one flies fast, flat and far. The gauge's top is a gold "strong" zone that
  lasts ≈0.6 s — released there the throw is full power with a small bonus (a gold flash, a distinct sound, a little
  faster and truer); held past it, his arm tires and the aim begins to sway gently (he can still throw).
* **Aim:** the reticle is where a full-power stone goes (its drop at that distance is allowed for); a weaker stone falls
  short of it, more the weaker it is. Wind (round 2) drifts the stone a little — aim into it; moving targets need a lead.
  Hit zones are a little forgiving. Aim assist only as a small magnetism on touch screens.
* **Challenge without frustration:** an average player clears every round within its stones; the rating (one to three
  marks) is where the challenge lives (stones used, strong releases, streaks, time). Round 4's cord stays the finale —
  hard but reachable.
* Phones: the same — hold the sling button to whirl, drag to aim, lift to throw; the gauge large and clear.
